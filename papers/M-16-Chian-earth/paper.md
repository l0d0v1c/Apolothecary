# M-16. Chian earth (Χία γῆ; *terra Chia*): state of the art

*The Apolothecary — state-of-the-art review. Version 1.0, 22 November 2026. Project DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.24422717](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24422717).*

**Certainty:** Unknown — three ancient authors agree that Chian earth was a white or ash-grey, flaky, moulded earth used as a mild detergent and cosmetic, but no ancient sample, no extraction site and no mineralogical identification exist in the literature found. · **What is lost:** identity, deposit and processing.

## Abstract

Chian earth (Χία γῆ, *Chia terra*) is one of the minor medicinal earths of the Aegean. Three ancient witnesses were found and checked against online critical texts.

- Dioscorides (5.155 Wellmann): the earth is white and ash-coloured, like Samian earth, flaky (πλακώδης), and differs from it in the shapes into which it is moulded. It tightens and brightens the skin of the face and body, and serves instead of natron in the bath.
- Pliny (*NH* 35.194): it is whitish, with the same effects as Samian earth, and is used above all on women's skin.
- Galen (*De simplicium medicamentorum temperamentis ac facultatibus* 9.1.4; Kühn XII.180–182): it is a mild detergent that some women put on their faces, and an excellent drug for burns.

The modern literature is thin. Christidis (1992) described a Lower–Middle Miocene bentonite near Thymiana in south-eastern Chios. It is grey, in places green, hard, gritty and opal-bearing, made of smectite with dolomite and opal-CT. No study links this or any other Chian deposit to the ancient earth, and no study has characterised a sample of the ancient product. This review sets out what the texts allow and proposes how the question could be tested.

## 1. Introduction

The Greek and Roman pharmacopoeia used a series of earths named after their places of origin. Dioscorides treats the Eretrian, Samian, Chian, Selinusian and Cimolian earths in consecutive chapters (5.152–156), and Pliny treats much the same group (*NH* 35.191–196). Some of them have recently been studied by combining texts, geology and laboratory testing. Hall and Photos-Jones (2008) argued that Lemnian earth "the artefact" was not the same as Lemnian earth "the raw material". Photos-Jones et al. (2015) tested Samian earth for antibacterial properties. Chian earth has had no comparable study in the literature found for this review. The dictionary entry (version 0.3) was a stub. This article therefore does three things. It assembles the ancient evidence, which turns out to be consistent and informative. It reports the only geological work on Chian clays that could be found and read. And it states plainly that the link between the two has not been made.

The name must be kept apart from two other Chian products. The "Chian stone" of Theophrastus (*De lapidibus* 6–7, 33) is a dark or black stone from the Chian quarries (Caley and Richards 1956, 71). Chian mastic (Galen, *Simpl. med.* 7.12.6; Pliny, *NH* 12.72) is a resin, not an earth.

## 3. Proposed identifications

No modern identification of Chian earth with a specific mineral or deposit was found in the literature consulted. Several features of the ancient testimony nevertheless constrain any future identification.

- **Colour.** The earth is white and ash-grey (Dioscorides 5.155) or "whitish" (*candicans*, Pliny, *NH* 35.194).
- **Texture.** It is "flaky" (πλακώδης) and "fine" (λεπτή) (Dioscorides 5.155).
- **Processing.** It "differs in the shapes of its moulding" (τοῖς σχήμασι τῆς ἀναπλάσεως, Dioscorides 5.155). The traded product was therefore re-formed into shaped pieces, as Pliny's general recipe for medicinal earths also implies (*NH* 35.193). This places Chian earth with the processed earths. For Lemnian earth, Hall and Photos-Jones (2008) argued that the product and the raw material should not be assumed to be the same.
- **Function.** It is a detergent and cosmetic: it substitutes for natron in the bath, is moderately "rhyptic", and brightens and tightens the skin (Dioscorides 5.155; Galen, *Simpl. med.* 9.1.4). It is also a burn remedy (Galen, *Simpl. med.* 9.1.4). These properties are attributed equally to Samian and Selinusian earth by all three authors. Their identity of effect is a pharmacological classification, and it is not evidence that the three materials were mineralogically alike.

**The Chios bentonite.** The dictionary entry pointed to Christidis's doctoral thesis (1992) on the bentonites of Milos, Kimolos and Chios. The thesis does describe a bentonite on Chios. It is associated with Neogene rocks cropping out in the south-east of the island, south of the village of Thymiana. Christidis (1992, 79) describes it as "a single tuffaceous horizon of Lower–Middle Miocene age which has been altered to bentonite", deposited in a fluvio-lacustrine environment, up to 8 m thick in outcrop. He continues: "a predominately grey (in places green), hard (probably due to the presence of opal) bentonite with abundant grit content", with no sign of hydrothermal alteration. Its mineralogy has two assemblages (Christidis 1992, 100):

- in the lower part, smectite with dolomite and opal-CT, plus minor quartz, plagioclase and calcite;
- in the upper part, smectite, quartz and plagioclase as major components, with chlorite, mica and serpentine as minor components, and talc and dolomite in traces.

Christidis (1992, 20) also cites earlier work. Andronopoulos identified the smectite as Ca-montmorillonite, and Kanaris thought the bentonite extended over a considerable area. The thesis makes no connection with the ancient Chian earth.

A raw material of this kind could in principle be washed and moulded into a fine, pale, smectite-rich cosmetic clay. But the deposit as described differs from the ancient descriptions. It is grey or green, hard and gritty. Dioscorides' earth is white, ash-grey, flaky and fine. This review cannot say whether the Thymiana bentonite, a refined fraction of it, or some other Chian clay was the ancient earth. Nor was any study found that addresses the question.

## 4. Analytical and experimental evidence

None directly. No archaeological or museum sample labelled or identified as Chian earth was found in the literature consulted, and no laboratory study of Chian earth as a material was found.

The indirect evidence is of two kinds.

1. **The geology of the Chios bentonite.** Christidis (1992) reports XRD and other analyses of the Thymiana bentonite (see §3). The summary of his thesis adds that the Greek bentonites studied as a group have good swelling properties after sodium activation, and cation exchange capacities between 40 and 105 meq/100 g. Those with low CEC contain abundant opal-CT. These properties were measured for industrial applications, not for comparison with an ancient cosmetic.
2. **Methods developed on related earths.** Laboratory testing of Dioscorides' earths has been applied to Samian earth (Photos-Jones et al. 2015). A combined textual and geochemical approach has been set out for Lemnian earth (Hall and Photos-Jones 2008). Photos-Jones and Hall (2011) wrote a general guide to the earths of the Aegean. This book could not be consulted for this review, so whether and how it discusses Chian earth is not reported here.

## 5. Discussion: what remains lost

Everything material is lost. The texts give a coherent picture of a pale, flaky, moulded detergent earth from Chios, used for the skin and for burns. They do not give:

- the place of extraction;
- the mineral composition;
- the method of preparation beyond moulding (Dioscorides 5.155) and the generic washing and baking (Pliny, *NH* 35.193).

The one studied Chian clay deposit (Christidis 1992) is a possible starting point. Nothing yet connects it to the ancient product, and its described appearance does not obviously match.

The literature on this entry is thin. Apart from the ancient texts and one geological thesis, no study was found that addresses Chian earth. This absence is itself the main result of the review. It is possible that relevant work exists in Greek-language geological or archaeological publications not indexed in the sources searched (Crossref, general web search). This review did not search them systematically.

## 6. Open questions and proposed research

1. **Complete the passages.** Search the TLG and PHI for Χία γῆ / Χίας γῆς / *Chia terra*. This would cover Oribasius, Aetius, Paul of Aegina and the Latin medical writers, to see whether later authors add provenance, preparation or appearance.
2. **Survey white clays on Chios.** Map and sample the Thymiana bentonite (Christidis 1992) and any other pale clays on the island, especially those that are white, flaky and fine-grained.
3. **Refine and compare.** Wash, levigate and mould samples according to Pliny, *NH* 35.193. Compare colour, texture and behaviour in water with Dioscorides' description (5.155). Test detergency on skin-like substrates and on wool against natron.
4. **Compare with Samian and Selinusian earths.** All three ancient authors equate the effects of the Chian, Samian and Selinusian earths. Comparing candidate materials from the three islands mineralogically and functionally, using the methods applied to Samian earth (Photos-Jones et al. 2015), would test whether the equivalence reflects similar clays or only similar uses.
5. **Look for the product.** Search museum collections and excavation reports from Chios for moulded earth pieces, the "shapes of moulding" in Dioscorides 5.155, of the kind known for Lemnian earth tablets (Hall and Photos-Jones 2008).
